CHAPTER I
INTRODUCTION
In today’s business environment, customer expectations continue to change as digital services become more common across different industries (Mordor Intelligence, 2025). Customers in the car rental sector increasingly prefer fast transactions, convenient booking processes, and accessible platforms that allow them to reserve vehicles more efficiently (IMARC Group, 2025). The growing use of smartphones and digital wallets in the Philippines has also contributed to the shift toward digital-first rental transactions (Mordor Intelligence, 2025). Because of this, car rental businesses are encouraged to adopt more responsive and technology-based systems to improve service quality and meet changing customer needs (IMARC Group, 2025).
Despite these developments, many car rental businesses still encounter operational difficulties because of manual processes and limited digital integration (USTP, 2024). Common challenges include disorganized record-keeping, delayed reservation confirmation, and difficulty in monitoring vehicle availability (USTP, 2024). Businesses may also experience problems in tracking rented vehicles in real time, which can affect supervision, service reliability, and vehicle security (IARJSET, 2024). The absence of organized maintenance monitoring may further create difficulty in managing the condition and readiness of rental units (RentSyst, 2026). In addition, manually fixed rental rates may limit the ability of a business to respond to demand, rental duration, and vehicle availability more effectively (Kannan and Kumar, 2026). These issues create operational gaps that may affect both the business and its customers, especially when faster, more accurate, and more flexible services are needed (Nomora, 2026).
To address these concerns, several studies and related systems emphasize the importance of integrating digital booking, real-time tracking, maintenance monitoring, geomapping, and dynamic pricing into rental operations (Kannan and Kumar, 2026). Research shows that real-time tracking and geofence-based monitoring can improve vehicle visibility, boundary control, and fleet security (IARJSET, 2024). Mobile-first booking platforms also help make rental services more accessible and organized for customers (Rent Centric, 2025). Systems with GPS-enabled fleet tracking and maintenance monitoring provide better administrative control over rental assets and vehicle conditions (RentSyst, 2026). Local systems with live GPS tracking further show that geomapping can improve customer confidence and vehicle security in real operational settings (Anis Transport Real-Time Tracking, 2025).
In response to these conditions, the researchers propose the development of a Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping for JD Car Rental. The proposed system aims to improve booking and rental management, payment recording, vehicle monitoring, and maintenance scheduling through a centralized digital platform. It also seeks to provide dynamic pricing and geomapping features that may help improve operational efficiency, vehicle security, and service quality. Through this study, the researchers aim to provide a more organized, modernized, and reliable system that will support the business operations of the beneficiary.
1.1 Background of the Study
Jennifer Morales, the proprietor of JD Car Rental, which opened for business on June 10, 2024, will benefit from this study. Customers that require short-term transportation for travel, personal, or other reasons can rent cars from the company. Currently, JD Car Rental manages its day-to-day operations manually. Customers who wish to rent a car manually submit questions, and available units are listed on the company’s Facebook page. Logbooks are also used to record business transactions and rental information. For a small business, this approach is still feasible, but as the number of clients and transactions rises, it can become less successful.
Manual processing in car rental operations may create several challenges for the business. Since booking details, customer records, and rental schedules are not stored in one centralized digital system, it may become difficult to organize transactions properly and monitor the status of each unit. The business may also encounter delays in responding to customer inquiries and confirming reservations. This suggests that a more structured and technology-based system may better support the operational needs of JD Car Rental.
Another important concern in car rental businesses is vehicle monitoring and security. Without a digital tracking system, the owner may have limited ability to monitor the real-time location and movement of rented cars. This may affect the security of the vehicles and reduce the business’s ability to respond quickly in case of emergencies, misuse, or delays in return. In the same way, integrating geomapping and real-time tracking in the proposed system may help JD Car Rental manage its units more effectively.
Another feature explored in this study is dynamic pricing. In most traditional rental businesses, prices are fixed and set manually by the owner, which works but is not always the most efficient approach. Pricing becomes smarter and more responsive when it takes into account factors like demand, usage patterns, and other conditions that influence rental decisions. Dynamic pricing is not just a technical upgrade but a practical way to make rental services more competitive and adaptable to real-world conditions.
Because of these existing conditions, the researchers propose the development of a Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping for JD Car Rental. The proposed system is intended to improve booking and rental management, payment recording, vehicle monitoring, maintenance scheduling, and security through mobile and web access. By integrating these features into one system, the study hopes to provide a more organized, efficient, and reliable solution for the business.

1.3 Scope and Limitations
This study focuses on the design and development of a Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping for JD Car Rental. The system is a web and mobile-based platform intended for the business’s internal rental operations and authorized users. The Booking and Rental Management feature allows customers to reserve vehicles with or without a driver provided by JD Car Rental, and enables the owner to process rentals, assign drivers when requested, monitor vehicle availability, and manage rental schedules in a more organized and centralized manner. The Dynamic Pricing feature provides flexible rental rates that adjust based on selected factors such as vehicle demand, rental duration, and unit availability, allowing the business to respond more effectively to varying rental conditions. The Real-Time Tracking and Monitoring feature enables the owner to track the live location and movement of rented vehicles, improving supervision, operational control, and the ability to respond to incidents during the rental period. The Asset and Maintenance Management feature allows the owner to record vehicle details and monitor maintenance schedules, repair status, and overall unit condition to ensure that rental units remain ready and properly maintained. The Geomapping feature displays vehicle locations on a map interface and applies geofence-based boundary monitoring to strengthen vehicle security and alert the owner when a vehicle moves outside a defined area. The Payment Method feature allows the recording and management of payment transactions related to rentals, providing a more organized and traceable record of customer payments. The Mobile and Web Access feature enables the system to be accessed through both mobile devices and web browsers, making it more convenient for the owner and authorized users to manage operations at any time. The Security feature ensures that system access is restricted to authorized users only, protecting rental records, customer information, and system data from unauthorized access. The Generation of Reports feature produces summaries of booking transactions, rental records, payment history, vehicle status, and maintenance schedules to support business monitoring and informed decision-making.
While the system offers several helpful features, there are some limitations to consider. The system does not cover other business functions such as accounting, payroll, and full financial management, as these fall outside the scope of rental operations management. The real-time tracking and geomapping features are dependent on GPS access, stable internet connection, and device compatibility, and the study does not aim to resolve connectivity or hardware limitations that may affect tracking performance during actual use. The dynamic pricing feature is based only on the pricing rules defined within the system, and the study does not aim to develop advanced predictive models or artificial intelligence-based pricing algorithms. The system does not include route optimization, traffic-based navigation, or fuel consumption analysis, as the tracking feature is limited to location monitoring and geofence-based alerts only. The system is specifically designed for JD Car Rental based on its existing operations and specific business needs, and it does not aim to provide a solution applicable to other car rental businesses without further modification.

1.5 Definition of Terms
The following key terms have been operationally defined in relation to their specific usage in this study. These definitions aim to provide clarity and ensure a consistent understanding of concepts central to the study.
Asset and Maintenance Management. In this study, it refers to a system feature that records vehicle information and monitors maintenance schedules, repair status, and overall unit condition to ensure that rental vehicles remain ready and properly maintained.
Booking. In this study, it refers to the process by which a customer reserves a vehicle for rental on a specified date and time through the system.
Car Rental Management System. In this study, it refers to the web and mobile-based platform developed for JD Car Rental that integrates booking and rental management, dynamic pricing, real-time tracking, asset and maintenance monitoring, geomapping, payment management, and report generation into a single centralized system.
Dynamic Pricing. In this study, it refers to a pricing feature of the system in which rental rates adjust based on selected factors such as vehicle demand, rental duration, and unit availability to allow the business to respond more effectively to varying rental conditions.
Generation of Reports. In this study, it refers to a system feature that produces summaries of booking transactions, rental records, payment history, vehicle status, and maintenance schedules to support business monitoring and decision-making.
Geofencing. In this study, it refers to a location-based security feature of the system that establishes a virtual boundary and alerts the owner when a rented vehicle moves outside the defined area.
Geomapping. In this study, it refers to a system feature that displays the real-time location of rented vehicles on a map interface to support location-based monitoring and vehicle security.
Maintenance Schedule. In this study, it refers to the planned schedule recorded in the system for checking, servicing, or repairing a rental vehicle.
Mobile and Web Access. In this study, it refers to the capability of the system to be accessed and used through both mobile devices and web browsers, allowing authorized users to manage rental operations at any time and from any compatible device.
Payment Method. In this study, it refers to a system feature that records and manages payment transactions made by customers in relation to their rental agreements.
Real-Time Tracking. In this study, it refers to a system feature that monitors the live location and movement of rented vehicles to improve supervision and operational control during the rental period.
Rental Transaction. In this study, it refers to the complete process of renting a vehicle as recorded in the system, including booking, approval, payment, vehicle use, and return.
Security. In this study, it refers to a system feature that restricts access to authorized users only and protects rental records, customer information, and system data from unauthorized access.
Vehicle Availability. In this study, it refers to the current status of a vehicle as recorded in the system, which may be classified as available, reserved, rented, or under maintenance.
Web-Based System. In this study, it refers to the component of the proposed system that can be accessed using a web browser through an internet connection, intended primarily for the owner and authorized administrative users 
CHAPTER II
Review of Related Literature/Studies/System
This chapter presents a review of related literature, studies, and existing systems relevant to the development of the proposed Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping for JD Car Rental. The purpose of this review is to examine existing research and technological solutions that address challenges in vehicle rental management, digital booking, real-time fleet tracking, dynamic pricing, and geomapping in both foreign and local contexts. The reviewed literature provides theoretical and practical foundations that guide the design and development of the proposed system.
2.1 Foreign Literature
Integrating Pricing and Capacity Decisions in Car Rental
Oliveira et al. (2025) studied how car rental profitability depends on the integration of fleet capacity and pricing strategies. Their research, published in ResearchGate, proposed a mathematical optimization model that treats demand as price-sensitive. The study highlights that by adjusting prices based on the return of capacity — where a vehicle becoming available immediately triggers a price recalibration — companies can solve the idle fleet problem and increase revenue per available car (RevPAC) without relying on complex predictive models. This study is relevant to the proposed system as it supports the concept of a capacity-aware dynamic pricing engine that adjusts rental rates based on real-time vehicle availability.
Origin-Based Differentiated Pricing for Vehicle Sharing
A 2024 research paper from EconStor explores origin-based pricing as a practical solution for vehicle sharing systems. Instead of complex forecasting, the system uses discrete price sets based on the location and time a rental starts. This approach incentivizes users to rent from low-demand zones by offering lower rates, effectively using pricing as a tool for user-based relocation to balance fleet distribution across geographic areas. This study supports the location-aware pricing module of the proposed system, which adjusts rental rates based on vehicle availability and demand.
Geofence-Based Vehicle Tracking and Security Systems
Research published in the International Academic Journal of Science and Engineering (IARJSET, 2024) emphasizes that ensuring vehicle safety through real-time location tracking has become a priority in fleet management. The study demonstrated a system using hardware modules to provide continuous vehicle monitoring and immediate alerts when a vehicle exits a predefined geographic boundary. The prototype achieved 95% precision in location tracking, proving its reliability for commercial theft prevention and boundary enforcement. This research is directly relevant to the geofence-based alert and boundary monitoring feature of the proposed system.
Rule-Based Dynamic Pricing and Secure Booking Platforms
Kannan and Kumar (2026), in a study published in the International Journal of Innovative Research in Technology, introduced a dual-layer pricing engine that uses rule-based guardrails combined with real-time data from external events such as weather and local news. This ensures that while prices remain dynamic, they always stay within a defined floor and ceiling, providing operational stability and transparency for rental administrators. This study provides the foundation for the rule-based pricing engine of the proposed system, which maintains defined minimum and maximum rate limits.
Revenue Management and Surge Pricing Metrics
According to Nomora (2026), modern rental pricing is shifting toward tiered rate structures that reflect actual demand. The study suggests that implementing a 20–30% weekend premium and weather-triggered price adjustments can boost revenue by 5–10%. The research emphasizes that the goal of dynamic pricing is not just to predict, but to manage utilization rates, keeping 70–85% of the fleet active at any given time. This study supports the demand-based tiered pricing system that maintains optimal fleet utilization through surge pricing metrics in the proposed system.
2.2 Local Literature
Philippines Car Rental and Leasing Market Outlook 2030
According to Mordor Intelligence (2025), the Philippine car rental market is valued at approximately USD 682.27 million and is projected to grow through 2030. The growth is heavily driven by a rapid shift to digital-first super-app bookings. The report notes that online bookings are forecast to advance rapidly as smartphone penetration and digital wallet usage, such as GCash and Maya, continue to normalize across the country. This literature reinforces the importance of developing a mobile-first booking platform with integrated digital payment options for local users, as implemented in the proposed system.
Digital Transformation and User Satisfaction in Local Rentals
The IMARC Group (2025) reports that the Philippine car rental market is increasingly adopting digital channels to improve customer satisfaction. In 2025, the adoption of digital car rental sites gained traction as users moved away from traditional counter-based transactions. This shift allows local operators to implement dynamic pricing models based on local usage patterns, ensuring rates remain competitive during peak local travel seasons. These findings support the digital booking interface with dynamic pricing features in the proposed system, tailored to local seasonal demand patterns.
Tourism-Driven Demand for Flexible Mobility
Data from the Department of Tourism (2024) indicates that the tourism sector accounts for a significant portion of the Philippine GDP. The record-high international arrivals in 2024 have reinforced the demand for flexible mobility. This trend necessitates systems that can handle varied road conditions and provide tourists with flexible, reliable transportation options through mobile-first interfaces. This literature supports the development of a mobile-first rental system in the proposed study, designed to accommodate flexible and multi-point transportation needs.
Operational Challenges in Local Fleet Management
A 2024 study from the University of Science and Technology of Southern Philippines (USTP) identified key operational challenges for local businesses, including vehicle availability imbalance and reservation errors. The researchers emphasized the need for real-time tracking to enhance security and reporting analytics to help owners optimize fleet utilization, especially for small-to-medium enterprises. These findings reinforce the development of a real-time fleet availability system with analytics reporting to reduce reservation errors and improve fleet utilization in the proposed system.
National Transportation Modernization and Fleet Safety
According to IMARC (2024), national agencies in the Philippines have reinforced safety and standardization across all transportation sectors. While car rentals are private, this initiative influences private sector choices by encouraging the introduction of safer and more standardized mobility solutions, pushing local operators to adopt advanced GPS and maintenance scheduling features. This literature supports the GPS-integrated maintenance scheduling module of the proposed system, aligned with national safety and fleet standardization standards.
2.3 Foreign Systems
Anolla 
Anolla is a foreign rental booking software that utilizes AI-powered automation and demand-based yield management. It supports dynamic pricing variations based on the day of the week and fleet availability and provides a data-driven rental flow that increases asset utilization. Anolla demonstrates how automated pricing adjustments can be integrated into a reservation platform. However, the system relies heavily on AI-based prediction models rather than rule-based configurations. In contrast, the proposed system adopts a rule-based dynamic pricing engine that gives administrators direct control over pricing parameters, making it more practical for small car rental businesses like JD Car Rental.
Nomora 
Nomora is a fleet integration platform that focuses on real-time visibility and advanced tracking. It integrates GPS modules with mobile-first fleet tools to provide administrators with immediate data on vehicle location and status. Nomora serves as a primary reference for integrating geomapping directly into a reservation dashboard. While Nomora is designed for large-scale fleet operations, the proposed system adapts these tracking and geomapping features to suit the operational scale of a local car rental business, allowing both the admin and customers to view vehicle locations in real time.
Rent Centric 
Rent Centric is a cloud-based solution designed specifically for car-sharing services and independent rental agencies. It features a robust customer self-service booking portal and mobile app support, providing the architectural basis for integrating mobile-based bookings with automated billing and invoicing systems. While Rent Centric is primarily built for car-sharing networks, the proposed system applies similar self-service booking and automated billing principles to a business-specific rental management platform, incorporating additional features such as dynamic pricing and geomapping.
RentSyst 
RentSyst is a car rental management platform known for its heavy integration of IoT technology. It provides GPS-enabled fleet tracking that gives owners full control over their assets and includes an analytics engine that helps small agencies automate document management and track maintenance. RentSyst serves as a model for the security and maintenance features of the proposed system. However, unlike RentSyst which is an off-the-shelf solution, the proposed system is specifically designed and configured around the existing operations and requirements of JD Car Rental.
Fleetio 
Fleetio is a specialized fleet management system that excels in maintenance tracking and predictive analytics. By using telematics data, it forecasts when vehicle components might fail, allowing repairs to be scheduled proactively. Fleetio provides a basis for the maintenance management module of the proposed system. While Fleetio focuses on predictive maintenance for large fleet organizations, the proposed system adapts these concepts into a simpler maintenance scheduling and monitoring module that suits the operational scale of a local small-to-medium car rental business.
2.4 Local Systems
Carbnb 
Carbnb is a prominent local peer-to-peer car rental marketplace in the Philippines. It facilitates bookings across major cities like Manila and Cebu and features integrated local payment methods, including GCash and credit cards. Carbnb demonstrates the successful implementation of a mobile-first interface tailored to the preferences of Filipino renters. However, Carbnb operates as a peer-to-peer marketplace and does not include features for internal fleet maintenance monitoring, geofencing, or dynamic pricing configuration. The proposed system extends these functions by integrating fleet monitoring, geomapping, and a rule-based pricing engine designed specifically for a single business operator.
DOON PH 
DOON is a local car-sharing marketplace that provides fully insured rentals through an app-based ecosystem and features in-app messaging for direct communication between the vehicle owner and the renter. This system provides insights into how local rental platforms can support user communication and coordinate rental logistics digitally. While DOON focuses on a broad marketplace model, the proposed system applies similar in-app notification and communication features to a focused business management platform designed for JD Car Rental's specific operational needs.
Toyota RentaCar x JoyRide 
This local system is a partnership between Toyota Motor Philippines and the JoyRide super-app, allowing users to book various vehicle types for self-driven use. It utilizes Fleet 360 GPS technology for real-time location monitoring and offers a local benchmark for integrating high-level security features into a consumer-facing app. While this system benefits from the resources of large corporate organizations, the proposed system adopts comparable real-time GPS tracking and geomapping features, adapted to the operational scale and budget of a small local car rental business.
Saferide Local Booking System 
Saferide is a local online rental booking system that utilizes a Book Early, Pay Less pricing logic to manage demand. It allows users to view real-time availability and select specific vehicle models, serving as a basis for the dynamic pricing engine in the proposed study. While Saferide focuses primarily on demand-based early-booking incentives, the proposed system incorporates a more comprehensive pricing engine that accounts for vehicle demand, rental duration, and availability, alongside booking management and fleet monitoring features.
Anis Transport Real-Time Tracking 
Anis Transport is a local rental provider that has integrated a 24/7 web-based booking portal with live GPS tracking, allowing both the administrator and the customer to view vehicle locations in real-time. This provides a practical local example of how geomapping improves customer confidence and vehicle security in real operational settings. The proposed system applies this same dual-view real-time geomapping concept while extending it further with geofence-based boundary monitoring, violation detection, and automated alert notifications for the admin.

3.1 Systems Development Method
The research methodology used in this study was the developmental and descriptive research method that utilized an iterative process of systems development. This approach is appropriate for the study because the proposed system was developed, designed, and evaluated based on the operational needs and processes of JD Car Rental.
The researchers used the developmental research design to develop, design, and evaluate the Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping to produce a functional and reliable system. The descriptive method was also applied in this study because it allows the researchers to gather and analyze data about the current operations of JD Car Rental, identify the existing problems, and determine the system requirements needed to address those problems.
To guide the development process, the researchers adopted the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) model. The Agile model was selected because it supports incremental and iterative development, allowing the researchers to refine system features based on continuous feedback and changing requirements throughout the development process. Its flexibility makes it well-suited for a system with multiple integrated features such as booking management, dynamic pricing, real-time tracking, geomapping, asset monitoring, and payment recording.
 
Figure 1 
Agile Methodology Model 
The figure above illustrates the Agile development model used in this study. The model consists of six iterative phases: Requirements, Design, Development, Testing, Deployment, and Review. Each phase was applied cyclically to ensure that the developed system met the functional and operational requirements of JD Car Rental.
Planning Phase
In this phase, the researchers gathered data to understand the current business operations of JD Car Rental and identify the system requirements. An online and face-to-face interview was conducted with Jennifer Morales, the proprietor of JD Car Rental, to examine how the business currently manages bookings, customer transactions, rental records, and vehicle monitoring. Since JD Car Rental currently relies on manual logbooks, Facebook-based inquiries, and informal communication to manage its operations, the interview provided the researchers with a clear understanding of the existing challenges and the functional requirements needed for the proposed system.
Based on the gathered data, the researchers identified the following key functional requirements for the proposed system: Booking and Rental Management, Dynamic Pricing, Real-Time Tracking and Monitoring, Asset and Maintenance Management, Geomapping, Payment Method, Mobile and Web Access, Security, and Generation of Reports. System diagrams including Context Diagrams, Data Flow Diagrams, Entity Relationship Diagrams, and Use Case Diagrams were also prepared during this phase to visually represent the system’s structure and data flow.
Design Phase
This phase focused on designing the overall structure and interface of the system based on the requirements gathered in the previous phase. The system has two main access interfaces — a web-based interface for the owner and administrator, and a mobile-based interface for customers. Figma was used to create the user interface mockups and wireframes. The system architecture follows a client-server model where the Flutter mobile app and React web client communicate with a centralized Laravel backend through RESTful APIs. The database structure was also planned using an Entity Relationship Diagram to define the data structures for customers, vehicles, bookings, payments, maintenance records, and pricing configurations stored in PostgreSQL.
Development Phase
This phase involved the development of the system following an iterative approach. For the web-based component, Laravel was used as the backend framework for its MVC architecture and built-in authentication, React was used for the frontend admin panel, and PostgreSQL served as the database for storing all system records. For the mobile component, Flutter was used to build a cross-platform application compatible with both Android and iOS. The Geomapping and Real-Time Tracking features were implemented using the Google Maps API and GPS-based location services, while the Dynamic Pricing feature was built using a rule-based pricing engine. Firebase Cloud Messaging was also integrated for real-time push notifications on geofence alerts and booking updates.
Testing Phase
The researchers are currently in this phase, conducting system testing iteratively to verify that all features function correctly and meet the defined requirements. Functional testing is being performed across all modules including Booking and Rental Management, Dynamic Pricing, Real-Time Tracking and Monitoring, Asset and Maintenance Management, Geomapping, Payment Method, Mobile and Web Access, Security, and Generation of Reports. The system is being evaluated by selected respondents representing both administrative and customer-level users, using a structured evaluation instrument based on the ISO 25010:2011 Software Quality Evaluation Criteria to assess the overall quality and usability of the system.
Deployment Phase
This phase covers the final implementation of the system following testing and approval. The Laravel backend will be deployed to a web server, the React web client will be made accessible to the admin and owner, and the Flutter mobile application will be distributed to customers on Android and iOS. A user orientation will be conducted to familiarize all users with the system’s key features, accompanied by documentation and user guides to support the transition from manual operations. Post-deployment monitoring and support will also be provided to address any issues that arise. This phase will be carried out after the system’s approval during the final defense.

3.10 System Evaluation Tools
 White Box Testing
White Box Testing will be used to evaluate the internal structure, functionality, and quality of the source code of the Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping. The researchers will use SonarQube as the static code analysis tool to identify possible bugs, vulnerabilities, code smells, and maintainability issues that may affect system performance and reliability. According to SonarSource (2024), SonarQube analyzes source code without executing the program to help ensure compliance with coding standards, software quality, and system security. Through this evaluation process, the researchers aim to ensure that the developed system is reliable, maintainable, secure, and properly structured before final implementation and evaluation.
 
Black Box Testing
Black Box Testing will be used to evaluate the functionality of the Mobile and Web-Based Car Rental Management System with Dynamic Pricing and Geomapping from the users’ perspective without examining the internal structure of the source code. The testing process will focus on evaluating system inputs, processes, outputs, and expected responses to determine whether the developed system performs according to the specified requirements.
